# Interpretation: ARIMA Cross-Domain Summary

Plain-language reading of `reports/arima/arima_cross_domain_summary.txt`,
following the same pattern as the `reports/eda/*_eda_interpretation.md`
files. Numbers below are taken directly from the summary as generated by
`src/arima_model.py`; see `spec/arima-fitting.md` /
`spec/arima-parameters.md` for the methodology.

## Column-by-column

- **`failed`** — whether order selection broke down (couldn't resolve `d`,
  or every grid candidate failed to converge). All four datasets are
  `False`: all four produced a usable model.
- **`n_train` / `n_test`** — size of the 80/20 split. `call_center_emea_2021_2025`
  is far smaller (209/53) because it's a *weekly* series over ~5 years,
  while the other three are *daily* series over ~4 years (1168/~293) —
  same split fraction, very different point counts.
- **`d`** — the differencing order chosen by the train-only ADF test. Only
  `generated_emergency_dept_stays` needed `d=1` (its level drifts / carries
  the planted annual cycle); the other three were already stationary at
  `d=0`.
- **`p_max` / `q_max`** — the ACF/PACF significance-based ceiling the grid
  search was allowed to search within. All four hit the global cap of 5 —
  every dataset had at least one "significant" lag past 5, which the grid
  search does not chase further (a documented limitation, not a bug — see
  `arima-parameters.md`'s "Global ceilings" section).
- **`order`** — the AIC-selected `(p,d,q)`:
  - `generated_job_shop_manufacturing` → **(1,0,1)** — matches its planted
    ground-truth ARMA(1,1) exactly, and agrees with
    `recover_structure.py`'s independently-computed recovery-validation
    AIC-preferred order. Strongest sanity check in the run.
  - `call_center_emea_2021_2025` → **(0,0,0)** — essentially "predict the
    training mean"; AIC found no autocorrelation structure worth modeling
    (consistent with its EDA description as a near-noise weekly series).
  - `generated_emergency_dept_stays` → **(5,1,1)** and
    `synthetic_it_support_tickets` → **(5,0,2)** — both maxed out `p` at
    the ceiling, i.e. ARIMA "reaching" for extra AR lags to approximate
    weekly pattern it has no seasonal term to capture directly (expected,
    per the locked plain-ARIMA-only decision in `arima-fitting.md`).
- **`aic_bic_agree`** — `False` only for `synthetic_it_support_tickets`,
  where BIC (heavier parameter-count penalty) preferred the trivial
  (0,0,0) instead of (5,0,2). AIC's pick is what's used, per the spec; the
  disagreement itself is informative — (5,0,2)'s extra complexity buys
  only a marginal AIC improvement over doing nothing.
- **`mae` / `rmse`** — **not comparable across rows**: each dataset's
  duration is in its own native unit (hours for IT-support, seconds for
  call-center, minutes for the two generated datasets). Call-center's MAE
  of ~56 looks huge next to job-shop's ~1.6, but that's seconds vs.
  minutes, not a real performance gap.
- **`mape`** — the one metric that *is* comparable across datasets
  (unit-independent). See `arima_mape_comparison.png` for the same
  ranking as a chart.

## Cross-domain MAPE ranking (best to worst)

| Rank | Dataset | MAPE | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `generated_job_shop_manufacturing` | 2.96% | Near-perfect recovery of a genuinely ARMA(1,1) process — the domain plain ARIMA is best suited to. |
| 2 | `generated_emergency_dept_stays` | 7.66% | Seasonal domain; ARIMA(5,1,1) partially compensates via extra AR lags but visibly smooths through weekly spikes (see forecast plot). |
| 3 | `call_center_emea_2021_2025` | 10.72% | Near-noise series; ARIMA degenerates to a constant-mean model, so its error reflects the series' own volatility more than a modeling shortfall. |
| 4 | `synthetic_it_support_tickets` | 16.68% | Weakest fit; AIC/BIC disagreement on the order suggests the additional AR/MA terms add little real structure. |

## RQ2 relevance

This ranking is the first direct evidence for RQ2 ("how varied is ARIMA's
performance across domains with differing temporal properties"): plain
ARIMA performs best in the domain with strong, simple persistence
structure and no seasonality (job-shop), and worst in domains where either
seasonality is present but unmodeled (ED, call-center) or the underlying
structure is weak/noisy (IT-support). This is consistent with the locked
design decision (no SARIMA) — the underperformance on seasonal domains is
an anticipated finding, not a defect to fix by changing model class after
the fact.
